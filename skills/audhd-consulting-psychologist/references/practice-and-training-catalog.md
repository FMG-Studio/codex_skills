# Practice and training evidence catalog

Last reviewed: 2026-08-08.

Use this catalog when the user asks for tools, exercises, accommodations, training, planning systems, stress or trauma practices, executive-function support, or transferable astronaut skills. Each record remains separate so population and evidence limits are not erased by synthesis.

## Reading rules

- `Direct AuDHD` means the study actually examined people with co-occurring autism and ADHD. Most records below do not.
- `Autism transfer` and `ADHD transfer` mean possible application to an AuDHD formulation, not proof for AuDHD.
- `General transfer` and `space/team transfer` are farther extrapolations and must be labeled as such.
- A source may support offering an experiment without proving the exact conversational adaptation, dose, or outcome for this user.
- Do not turn symptom improvement into quality-of-life improvement, association into causation, mechanistic plausibility into treatment, or a selected astronaut sample into a clinical population.

## Co-occurring autism and ADHD

### P01 — Integrated AuDHD assessment and care

- Source: Young et al. (2020), expert consensus. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7247165/>
- Population/design: people with co-occurring autism and ADHD across the lifespan; consensus rather than intervention trial.
- Finding: both conditions, their interaction, impairment, comorbidity, communication needs, and treatment tolerability should be considered together.
- Usable approach: integrated formulation and coordinated care rather than forcing one-condition explanations.
- Limit: does not establish the efficacy of a specific adult AuDHD exercise.

### P02 — Multimodal AuDHD interventions in younger populations

- Source: 2025 systematic review. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12156790/>
- Population/design: children and adolescents with co-occurring ADHD and autism.
- Finding: maps multimodal pharmacological, behavioral, educational, and family approaches.
- Usable approach: generate questions about coordination and environmental support.
- Limit: pediatric evidence must not be presented as adult intervention evidence.

## Autistic-adult psychological support and adaptation

### P03 — Fit-for-purpose psychological interventions

- Source: Deakin et al. (2026), systematic review of 69 papers. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13180214/>
- Population/design: autistic adults; most studies pilot or exploratory.
- Finding: mindfulness and CBT were common; useful adaptations repeatedly included concrete language, visual supports, concise material, and autism-experienced therapists. Only a minority used autism-valid measures.
- Usable approach: adapt delivery, pacing, language, communication channel, and outcome measures.
- Limit: heterogeneous early evidence; no universal intervention or adaptation package was established.

### P04 — NICE adult-autism care adaptations

- Source: NICE CG142, reviewed in 2025. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/Recommendations>
- Population/design: clinical guideline for autistic adults.
- Finding: adapt care to communication and individual needs; assess differential and coexisting conditions; use condition-specific guidance when appropriate.
- Usable approach: accessible language, predictable structure, environmental adaptation, and collaborative care.
- Limit: a guideline recommendation is not direct evidence that every accommodation improves every outcome.

### P05 — French autonomy and quality-of-life guidance

- Source: Haute Autorite de Sante adult-autism recommendations. <https://www.has-sante.fr/jcms/c_2828597/fr/autisme-de-l-adulte-tout-mettre-en-oeuvre-pour-l-autonomie-l-inclusion-sociale-et-la-qualite-de-vie>
- Population/design: French professional and social-care guidance for autistic adults.
- Finding: interventions should be individualized, environment-aware, rights-based, and decided with the autistic adult.
- Usable approach: shared decisions, autonomy support, environmental change, and quality-of-life goals.
- Limit: not an efficacy trial and not specific to AuDHD.

### P06 — German S3 autism treatment guideline

- Source: AWMF S3 guideline, part 2. <https://www.awmf.org/aktuelles/awmf-aktuell/autismus-spektrum-stoerungen-im-kindes-jugend-erwachsenenalter-teil-2-therapie>
- Population/design: German evidence and consensus guideline across the lifespan, including adults and coexisting conditions.
- Finding: distinguishes supported, consensus-based, and harmful approaches and routes coexisting disorders to appropriate condition-specific care.
- Usable approach: match intervention to the actual target rather than treating autism as a single symptom to remove.
- Limit: lifespan recommendations vary in adult applicability; inspect the relevant section before use.

### P07 — Occupational-therapy practice guidance

- Source: 2024 occupational-therapy practice guideline based on 48 systematic reviews. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38758762/>
- Population/design: autistic people across participation domains.
- Finding: supports neurodiversity-affirming, trauma-informed, strengths- and interests-aware occupational practice.
- Usable approach: analyze person, activity, environment, access, and participation together.
- Limit: the evidence behind particular adult techniques varies and should be checked separately.

### P08 — Autistic burnout

- Source: Ali et al. (2025), systematic review of 48 studies and about 4,000 participants. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41207162/>
- Population/design: autistic people; samples were often white, female, and late identified.
- Finding: reported contributors included sensory and social overwhelm, masking, stigma, demands, and alexithymia; reported supports included rest, solitude, sensory relief, self-understanding, and individual or community support.
- Usable approach: demand reduction, sensory relief, recovery time, and sustainable-capacity tracking.
- Limit: burnout is not a formal diagnosis; qualitative associations do not establish treatment effects.

### P09 — Workplace accommodations

- Source: 2026 systematic review of 10 studies from 2015–2025. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805453/>
- Population/design: autistic employees; technological, environmental, supervisory, and psychosocial accommodations.
- Finding: accommodations were associated with retention, satisfaction, and productivity.
- Usable approach: written priorities, predictable check-ins, environmental control, assistive technology, and supervisor support.
- Limit: small observational evidence base; causality and universal fit are not established.

### P10 — Physical workplace adjustments

- Source: 2024 systematic review. <https://doi.org/10.1111/apps.12431>
- Population/design: workplace environment research relevant to autistic workers.
- Finding: control over environment, private space, and sensory adjustments were recurring facilitators.
- Usable approach: quieter location, light/noise control, retreat space, remote or flexible arrangements.
- Limit: workplace and individual contexts differ; treat each change as a reviewed accommodation.

## Adult ADHD psychological and executive-function interventions

### P11 — Broad adult ADHD intervention comparison

- Source: Ostinelli et al. (2025), component network meta-analysis of 113 randomized trials. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39701638/>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; pharmacological, psychological, and neurostimulatory interventions.
- Finding: the strongest short-term symptom effects were pharmacological; CBT had some support, while longer-term and quality-of-life evidence was thinner.
- Usable approach: offer ADHD-adapted CBT skills and track function and quality of life separately from symptoms.
- Limit: never convert group medication evidence into personal prescribing; intervention rankings do not establish AuDHD fit.

### P12 — Non-pharmacological adult ADHD network meta-analysis

- Source: 2025 systematic review and network meta-analysis, 37 studies and 2,289 adults. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11825462/>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; CBT, cognitive therapy, mindfulness-based cognitive therapy, neurofeedback, non-invasive brain stimulation, exercise, self-alert training, Tai Chi, supplements, and working-memory training.
- Finding: short-term benefits were reported for CBT, cognitive therapy, mindfulness-based therapy, non-invasive stimulation, and physical exercise, with heterogeneity and certainty limitations.
- Usable approach: prioritize lower-risk behavioral and cognitive methods whose target matches the user's difficulty.
- Limit: do not label every listed intervention effective, equivalent, durable, or tested in AuDHD.

### P13 — ADHD umbrella review

- Source: BMJ (2025), umbrella review of 221 meta-analyses. <https://www.bmj.com/content/391/bmj-2025-085875>
- Population/design: ADHD interventions across ages; umbrella review and decision platform.
- Finding: adult evidence supported several medication classes and CBT for symptom efficacy; many non-drug findings had low or very-low certainty.
- Usable approach: calibrate confidence and discuss tradeoffs rather than presenting a long menu as equally proven.
- Limit: umbrella reviews inherit limitations and overlap of included meta-analyses; no personal medication advice.

### P14 — Metacognitive therapy for planning and organization

- Source: Solanto et al., randomized trial of 88 adults. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3633586/>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; 12-week manualized group metacognitive therapy versus supportive psychotherapy.
- Finding: training targeted time management, organization, and planning and outperformed the comparison on ADHD-related outcomes.
- Usable approach: one capture system, prioritization, task decomposition, time estimation, and weekly review.
- Limit: the manualized program is more than isolated hacks; a shortened chat adaptation is an experiment, not the tested intervention.

### P15 — CBT for adult ADHD

- Source: Cochrane review. <https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD010840_cognitive-behavioural-therapy-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-adhd-adults>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; CBT compared with active and inactive controls.
- Finding: CBT may improve core symptoms and some functioning, anxiety, or depression outcomes; evidence ranged from very low to moderate quality.
- Usable approach: behavioral skills plus examination of thoughts that block initiation or recovery from setbacks.
- Limit: benefit magnitude and durability remain uncertain; CBT is not a substitute for all other indicated care.

### P16 — Japanese adult-ADHD CBT synthesis

- Source: 2024 Japanese review. <https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjghp/36/3/36_200/_article/-char/ja>
- Population/design: Japanese professional review of executive-function models and international treatment guidance.
- Finding: psychoeducation and CBT focused on functional impairment and behavioral skill acquisition are recommended in international guidance.
- Usable approach: culturally and linguistically clear psychoeducation tied to real-life skills.
- Limit: narrative professional synthesis, not a new efficacy trial.

### P17 — Korean executive-function intervention review

- Source: 2025 systematic review focused on randomized controlled studies. <https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/mobile/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003279420>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; executive-function interventions.
- Finding: reviews intervention approaches targeting adult executive-function difficulties.
- Usable approach: use as a multilingual evidence map and inspect the underlying trials before a strong claim.
- Limit: the abstract/index record alone is insufficient for extracting a technique-specific effect size.

### P18 — DBT adapted for adult ADHD

- Source: 2025 meta-analysis of 8 RCTs and 850 participants. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2025.116551>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; DBT programs containing at least two skills modules.
- Finding: moderate pooled improvement in ADHD symptoms and improvement in quality of life versus controls.
- Usable approach: emotion regulation, distress tolerance, mindfulness, and behavioral analysis adapted to ADHD.
- Limit: intervention packages and comparators varied; this is ADHD rather than AuDHD evidence.

## Prospective memory, reminders, and future planning

### P19 — Implementation intentions in autistic adults

- Source: 2014 adult-autism study. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25151603/>
- Population/design: autistic adults; prospective memory and implementation-intention support.
- Finding: `if X, then Y` planning can support some prospective-memory tasks.
- Usable approach: bind one observable cue to one concrete action in the action context.
- Limit: effects are task- and context-specific and should not be generalized to all executive dysfunction.

### P20 — Episodic future thinking and everyday prospective memory

- Source: 2026 study of 196 adults, 98 with ADHD and 98 controls. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42475007/>
- Population/design: adults with and without ADHD; outcome- and process-focused future simulation.
- Finding: episodic future thinking improved everyday prospective memory; outcome focus was strongest overall, while process focus showed a moderate benefit only in the ADHD group.
- Usable approach: briefly imagine the actual future situation and either the completed outcome or the action sequence before setting the cue.
- Limit: first evidence from one study, not an established treatment protocol.

### P21 — Preliminary future-thinking evidence in ADHD

- Source: 2026 adult-ADHD study. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41684048/>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; episodic future thinking.
- Finding: imagining execution of a future action showed preliminary promise, with effects influenced by verbal ability.
- Usable approach: offer verbal, written, or visual rehearsal rather than assuming imagery works for everyone.
- Limit: preliminary and sensitive to individual cognitive differences.

### P22 — Cognitive offloading and external reminders

- Source: general-adult research synthesis. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11781985/>
- Population/design: adults in prospective-memory and cognitive-offloading research, not specifically AuDHD.
- Finding: external reminders can reduce internal memory demands and improve prospective action.
- Usable approach: place one reminder at the point of action, externalize future intentions, and review reminder usefulness.
- Limit: reminder overload, habituation, and real-world context can reduce benefit; this is general transfer evidence.

## Emotion regulation, distress tolerance, and mindfulness

### P23 — DBT for autistic adults with severe emotion dysregulation

- Source: 2025 randomized trial of 63 autistic adults without intellectual disability. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12083952/>
- Population/design: autistic adults with emotion dysregulation and self-harm or suicidality; 18-week DBT.
- Finding: emotion dysregulation improved and gains were sustained at follow-up.
- Usable approach: professionally delivered DBT; skills may include chain analysis, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness.
- Limit: high-risk population and full treatment package; do not substitute isolated chat exercises for treatment or crisis care.

### P24 — Pragmatic DBT trial for suicidality in autistic adults

- Source: 2024 pragmatic randomized trial. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38606582/>
- Population/design: autistic adults with suicidal behavior; DBT intervention.
- Finding: provides direct autism-specific evidence relevant to suicidality and associated outcomes.
- Usable approach: referral to adapted DBT when clinically appropriate and available.
- Limit: not a self-help protocol; imminent risk still requires emergency action.

### P25 — DBT meta-analysis in autism

- Source: 2026 meta-analysis searching English and Chinese databases. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42104500/>
- Population/design: randomized trials of DBT in autistic populations.
- Finding: pooled results were promising for emotion regulation, suicidal ideation, and depression.
- Usable approach: consider adapted DBT as a treatment option to discuss with a qualified clinician.
- Limit: few trials and heterogeneity prevent a universal recommendation.

### P26 — Mindfulness-based stress reduction for autistic adults

- Source: 2025 systematic review. <https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1490092>
- Population/design: autistic adults in mindfulness-based stress-reduction studies.
- Finding: promising effects were reported across small heterogeneous studies.
- Usable approach: brief, optional practices with external, movement, visual, or sound focus when internal focus is uncomfortable.
- Limit: do not force interoception, closed eyes, stillness, or mindfulness during dissociation or acute danger.

### P27 — Mindfulness and work performance

- Source: 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs. <https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.70123>
- Population/design: general academic and occupational populations.
- Finding: small post-intervention task-performance improvement versus passive controls, but no clear advantage versus active controls.
- Usable approach: optional stress-management experiment, not a productivity guarantee.
- Limit: general-population evidence and high heterogeneity; not specific to autism, ADHD, or AuDHD.

## Trauma and PTSD

### P28 — PTSD assessment and treatment in autistic people

- Source: 2024 systematic review. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13002758/>
- Population/design: autistic people; assessment and treatment literature.
- Finding: autism-specific treatment evidence was scarce; EMDR add-on evidence involved 21 adults, while adapted CBT, narrative exposure, and trauma-informed approaches were mostly small studies or reports. One exposure approach worsened avoidance.
- Usable approach: autism-informed trauma clinician, communication and sensory adaptations, consent, pacing, and stabilization.
- Limit: no do-it-yourself trauma exposure or memory processing from a conversational formulation.

### P29 — Complex PTSD clinical guideline

- Source: 2026 clinical guideline. <https://mentalhealth.bmj.com/content/28/1/e301158.full.pdf>
- Population/design: general complex-PTSD evidence, not autism-specific.
- Finding: trauma-focused therapies with or without exposure received recommendations whose strength was limited by certainty.
- Usable approach: route to qualified trauma treatment and adapt delivery to neurodivergent needs.
- Limit: general evidence cannot establish safety or efficacy of an unadapted protocol for an autistic or AuDHD person.

## Exercise, sleep, and physiological load

### P30 — Exercise and executive function in adult ADHD

- Source: 2025 systematic review. <https://doi.org/10.3390/psychiatryint6040120>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; exercise and executive-function outcomes.
- Finding: exercise was promising for some executive-function outcomes, with a limited and heterogeneous evidence base.
- Usable approach: individualized, accessible movement before or between high-load tasks as an experiment.
- Limit: not a substitute for ADHD treatment and not safe to prescribe without considering health and disability.

### P31 — Physical activity and adult ADHD symptoms

- Source: 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis. <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1755296625000560>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; physical-activity interventions.
- Finding: physical activity was associated with improvement in core symptoms; intervention duration predicted some reductions.
- Usable approach: regular tolerable movement with outcome and burden tracking.
- Limit: more rigorous trials are needed; no universal exercise type or dose follows from the review.

### P32 — Exercise and sleep in adult ADHD

- Source: 2025 meta-analysis, 131 participants. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40003220/>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; exercise and sleep outcomes.
- Finding: objective sleep-duration improvement showed a non-significant trend.
- Usable approach: track whether movement affects sleep for this person instead of promising improvement.
- Limit: small evidence base and non-significant primary objective-sleep finding.

### P33 — NASA fatigue countermeasures

- Source: NASA Fatigue Countermeasures Laboratory. <https://www.nasa.gov/human-systems-integration-division/human-performance/fatigue-countermeasures-laboratory/>
- Population/design: aviation, mission-control, astronaut, and analog operational research.
- Finding: circadian misalignment, acute and chronic sleep loss, and sleep inertia can impair alertness and performance; scheduling and light are studied as countermeasures.
- Usable approach: protect sleep opportunity, plan around predictable low-alertness periods, use light and scheduling carefully, and reduce safety-critical load when sleep deprived.
- Limit: operational research in selected personnel is not AuDHD treatment evidence; sleep problems may require medical assessment.

## Uncertainty and decision support

### P34 — Intolerance of uncertainty and anxiety in autism

- Source: systematic review and meta-analysis. <https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/140692/>
- Population/design: autistic populations; association between intolerance of uncertainty and anxiety.
- Finding: the constructs were associated, making uncertainty a plausible formulation target.
- Usable approach: make uncertainty explicit, separate known from unknown, define a reversible next experiment, and predefine a review point.
- Limit: association does not prove that a specific choice-reduction technique treats anxiety or AuDHD.

### P35 — Stress-arousal reappraisal

- Source: 2024 meta-analysis of randomized trials. <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-58408-w>
- Population/design: general adult performance tasks under stress.
- Finding: reappraising arousal showed small performance benefits, larger when combined with other content; effects varied by task.
- Usable approach: optionally frame arousal as information and pair it with a concrete plan, without denying distress or danger.
- Limit: never use mindset language to override overload, trauma, unsafe conditions, or the need for accommodation.

## Astronaut, expeditionary, and high-reliability skills

### P36 — NASA Human Factors and Behavioral Performance

- Source: NASA Human Research Program. <https://www.nasa.gov/reference/about-human-factors-and-behavioral-performance/>
- Population/design: astronauts, ground analogs, spacecraft and team systems.
- Finding: NASA studies sleep, cognition, team cohesion, performance, design, training, human-robot interaction, and psychological readiness under isolation and confinement.
- Usable approach: treat performance as a person-system problem involving environment, schedule, tools, team, and recovery.
- Limit: selected astronaut populations and mission risks are not clinical AuDHD populations.

### P37 — NASA expeditionary skills

- Source: NASA team-training material. <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220013497/downloads/Landon%20Bell%202022%20-%20DoD%206.1%20conf%2C%20space%20teams%20Rev%201.pdf>
- Population/design: astronaut expeditionary and crew-office training.
- Finding: five named competencies are self-care or self-management, leadership or followership, communication, team care, and teamwork or group living.
- Usable approach: daily self-check, explicit roles, closed-loop communication, mutual monitoring, and shared-living agreements.
- Limit: the source describes a training framework, not an RCT and not treatment for neurodevelopmental conditions.

### P38 — ESA CAVES behavioral training

- Source: European Space Agency. <https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/CAVES_and_Pangaea/What_is_CAVES>
- Population/design: 34 astronauts from six agencies trained in cave expeditions from 2011–2019.
- Finding: scenarios practiced communication, decision making, problem solving, leadership, and team dynamics.
- Usable approach: rehearse a bounded scenario, rotate leader and follower roles, and debrief what supported shared awareness.
- Limit: descriptive program evidence; stressful simulation must be voluntary, accessible, and safely dosed.

### P39 — ESA mission and emergency training

- Source: ESA increment-specific training. <https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Astronauts/Increment-specific_training>
- Population/design: astronauts assigned to ISS missions.
- Finding: mission-specific replicas and simulations are used to rehearse operations and station-wide emergencies.
- Usable approach: practice the exact environment, tool, cue, and stopping rule before a high-load real task.
- Limit: do not imitate emergency stress or deprivation; use low-intensity rehearsal appropriate to the user.

### P40 — Team dynamics in long-distance space analogs

- Source: 2019 systematic review. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31156490/>
- Population/design: teams in Antarctic, chamber, military, firehouse, and other long-distance-spaceflight analog environments.
- Finding: maps cohesion, conflict, roles, leadership, composition, and performance under isolation and confinement.
- Usable approach: monitor team process, role clarity, workload, conflict, and recovery rather than relying on personality labels.
- Limit: analogs differ substantially from spaceflight and everyday neurodivergent life.

### P41 — Spaceflight Standard Measures

- Source: 2025 multidisciplinary spaceflight monitoring study. <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-025-00532-6>
- Population/design: professional astronauts and standardized in-flight monitoring.
- Finding: the behavioral-health domain tracks sleep, circadian rhythm, workload, habitability, team function, and cognition; prior data in 25 astronauts found temporary slowing in processing speed, working memory, and sustained attention without widespread cognitive decline.
- Usable approach: establish a baseline and monitor several interacting performance conditions rather than interpreting one bad day as loss of ability.
- Limit: tiny selected samples and unique flight conditions; monitoring is not a therapeutic protocol.

### P42 — Cognitive aids in emergencies

- Source: systematic review of 13 randomized trials. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10107924/>
- Population/design: adult clinical emergencies in simulation or practice; algorithms, checklists, and decision aids.
- Finding: cognitive aids reduced missed care steps from 43.3% to 11%; evidence was rated moderate.
- Usable approach: short checklist for a recurring high-load sequence, familiarized and tested before it is needed.
- Limit: clinical-team emergency performance is general-transfer evidence; poorly designed checklists can add load.

### P43 — Crew resource management

- Source: 2019 systematic review of 61 healthcare CRM studies. <https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/9/2/e025247>
- Population/design: healthcare teams; aviation-derived human-factors training.
- Finding: recurring components included briefing, closed-loop communication, situational awareness, decision making, leadership, teamwork, and workload management, but interventions and reporting varied greatly.
- Usable approach: pre-task brief, explicit roles, call-and-response confirmation, workload redistribution, and post-task debrief.
- Limit: CRM is an umbrella rather than one reproducible intervention; evidence is not AuDHD-specific.

### P44 — NASA team-risk boundary

- Source: NASA Team Risk evidence page. <https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/esdmd/hhp/team-risk/>
- Population/design: human-spaceflight risk program.
- Finding: inadequate cooperation, coordination, communication, and psychosocial adaptation threaten mission performance; NASA also notes gaps in systematic team monitoring and evaluation.
- Usable approach: make communication and coordination observable and reviewable rather than assuming a team is functioning.
- Limit: NASA itself identifies evidence gaps; do not market expeditionary skills as fully validated clinical training.

## Additional current intervention and tool evidence

### P45 — Acute and chronic exercise in adult ADHD

- Source: 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41638541/>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; acute and chronic exercise studies examining executive functions and core symptoms.
- Finding: acute exercise showed preliminary benefits for some executive outcomes, while chronic-intervention findings were mixed.
- Usable approach: test one accessible bout before a cognitively demanding task and measure the immediate effect separately from any longer-term routine.
- Limit: preliminary evidence does not establish a universal intensity, duration, or durable ADHD effect.

### P46 — START exercise add-on trial

- Source: 2025 randomized controlled START study. <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1690216/full>
- Population/design: adults with ADHD; 12-week physical-exercise add-on intervention with a small and unequal analyzed sample.
- Finding: the intervention group reported reductions in ADHD symptoms and insomnia and improved self-rated health, while the small sample limits precision and generalization.
- Usable approach: structured, monitored exercise may be discussed as an adjunct when medically appropriate and acceptable.
- Limit: small trial, attrition and group-size imbalance; not evidence that exercise replaces established care or works in AuDHD.

### P47 — 2026 randomized-intervention synthesis in autism

- Source: 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis. <https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-026-00652-2>
- Population/design: randomized pharmacological and non-pharmacological autism intervention trials across heterogeneous populations and behavioral domains.
- Finding: behavioral and psychosocial interventions showed greater pooled efficacy for selected core-symptom outcomes, while design and context substantially influenced results.
- Usable approach: inspect the exact age, outcome, acceptability, and intervention before considering a transferable component.
- Limit: pooled core-symptom framing does not establish adult AuDHD wellbeing benefit or justify normalization goals.

### P48 — Project AIM early-childhood intervention meta-analysis

- Source: 2026 updated systematic review and secondary analysis. <https://www.bmj.com/content/393/bmj.s887>
- Population/design: autism interventions in early childhood.
- Finding: updates estimates and examines how study and outcome features affect reported intervention effects.
- Usable approach: use only to understand pediatric evidence quality and outcome-measure sensitivity when discussing broader claims.
- Limit: early-childhood findings are not adult or AuDHD practice evidence and must not drive adult conversational exercises.

### P49 — Optimal use of reminders

- Source: Gilbert et al. (2019), three general-population experiments. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31448938/>
- Population/design: adults choosing between unaided memory and external reminders under different reward conditions.
- Finding: reminder choice reflected confidence and effort as well as objective benefit; feedback and task difficulty shifted reminder bias.
- Usable approach: compare remembered actions with and without one reminder and calibrate the system from observed benefit rather than shame or intuition alone.
- Limit: laboratory metacognition research, not ADHD or autism treatment evidence.

### P50 — Excessive reminder use

- Source: Gilbert et al. (2020), general cognitive-offloading research. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33032027/>
- Population/design: adults using external reminders in laboratory tasks.
- Finding: people may offload more often than is objectively optimal, partly through confidence and effort-minimization processes.
- Usable approach: review whether each reminder triggers useful action or merely adds alerts, and remove or redesign low-value reminders.
- Limit: `optimal` in a laboratory reward task is not the same as accessible everyday support; do not use the result to shame reliance on aids.

## Applying a record

For one selected record, create a compact trial card:

```text
target:
evidence population:
tested intervention or observed support:
adaptation being tried:
smallest trial:
expected benefit:
burden and risks:
measure:
review date or cue:
continue, change, or stop rule:
```

Measure outcomes relevant to the user: initiation cost, missed intentions, overload, distress, recovery time, sleep, access to food and care, relationship impact, safety, participation, and quality of life. Do not judge success only by visible productivity or appearing less autistic or ADHD.
